Phishing

Deceptive emails and messages designed to steal your personal information.

Learn More
What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack that tricks you into giving up sensitive information like passwords or credit card numbers by pretending to be a trustworthy entity. Common signs include suspicious sender addresses, urgent language, and unexpected attachments.

Why it is dangerous: Falling for phishing can lead to identity theft, financial loss, and unauthorized access to your accounts.

How to protect yourself: Verify sender addresses before clicking links, look for spelling and grammar mistakes, and when in doubt, contact the organization directly.

🔒

Password Security

Creating and managing strong passwords to protect your accounts.

Learn More
Password Security

Strong password security is your first line of defense online. Use unique, complex passwords for each account and consider using a password manager to keep track of them securely.

Why it is dangerous: Weak or reused passwords can be easily compromised, leading to account takeovers and credential stuffing attacks.

How to protect yourself: Use at least 12 characters with mixed types, never reuse passwords across important accounts, and enable two-factor authentication wherever possible.

🦠

Malware

Malicious software that can damage or gain unauthorized access to your systems.

Learn More
Malware

Malware (malicious software) includes viruses, ransomware, spyware, and trojans that can infiltrate your devices. It often spreads through suspicious downloads, email attachments, or compromised websites.

Why it is dangerous: Malware can steal data, encrypt your files for ransom, monitor your activities, and create botnets used in further attacks.

How to protect yourself: Keep your software and antivirus updated, avoid downloading from untrusted sources, and be cautious with email attachments.

🧠

Social Engineering

Manipulating people into performing actions or divulging confidential information.

Learn More
Social Engineering

Social engineering exploits human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities. Attackers manipulate people into breaking normal security procedures. Common tactics include pretexting, baiting, and tailgating.

Why it is dangerous: Social engineering bypasses technical security by targeting human trust, potentially leading to unauthorized access, financial fraud, and data breaches.

How to protect yourself: Verify identities before sharing information, be skeptical of unsolicited requests, and educate yourself and others regularly.

🔐

Online Privacy

Protecting your personal data and understanding privacy settings.

Learn More
Online Privacy

Protecting your online privacy involves managing what personal information you share and who can access it. With proper privacy practices, you can reduce your digital footprint and minimize targeted advertising and profiling.

Why it is dangerous: Poor privacy practices can result in targeted advertising, identity theft, profiling, and personal data being sold or exploited by malicious actors.

How to protect yourself: Review and adjust social media privacy settings, use browser extensions that block trackers, and be mindful of what you share publicly.

🌐

Safe Browsing

Navigating the web securely and avoiding dangerous sites.

Learn More
Safe Browsing

Safe browsing practices help you avoid malicious websites, downloads, and online threats. Always check that websites use HTTPS and be cautious of unexpected pop-ups or warnings.

Why it is dangerous: Unsafe browsing can result in drive-by downloads, phishing sites, malware infections, and exposure to scams and fraud.

How to protect yourself: Look for the padlock icon and HTTPS in the address bar, avoid clicking on suspicious pop-ups, and use a reputable ad-blocker.

Security Tips

🔐

Use unique passwords for different accounts

Never reuse the same password across multiple sites. If one account is compromised, all your other accounts remain secure.

🆙

Enable two-factor authentication

Add an extra layer of security to your accounts. Even if someone steals your password, they can't access your account without the second factor.

🚫

Avoid suspicious links

Don't click on links in unexpected emails or messages. Hover over links to see the actual destination before clicking.

📱

Keep software updated

Regularly update your operating system, apps, and antivirus software. Updates often include critical security patches.

🙅

Never share sensitive information with strangers

Be wary of unsolicited requests for personal information, whether by email, phone, or message.

🌐

Check website URLs before entering personal information

Verify you're on the legitimate website before entering passwords or financial details. Look for HTTPS and the correct domain name.

📦

Avoid downloading files from untrusted sources

Only download files from official and trusted sources. Untrusted downloads can contain malware or spyware.

🔒

Use secure Wi-Fi connections

Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive transactions. Use a VPN if you must connect to public networks.
